# RA-02 — Member JTBD Interview Guide

**Version:** v0.1 draft — 2026-08-17
**Activity:** RA-02, NTUC Club Loyalty Member PWA research plan
**Gaps targeted:** G01 (what members are trying to achieve), G03 (does Tier 2 mean anything),
G09 (expiry and reversal acceptance)
**Hypotheses tested:** H002, H004, H006, H007, H008
**Assumptions under test:** A002 (segments), A005 (economics), A006 (union Tier 2)
**Format:** 60 minutes, 1:1, remote or on-site
**Participants:** 12–15 across Families, PMEs, DINKs/Silvers; ≥5 union members; ≥4 aged 60+

---

## Before you start

**This is not a feature interview.** The programme is already specified — 34 Phase 1
requirements, two tiers, a fixed earn rate. We are not asking members what to build. We are
finding out what they are trying to get done when they visit, so we can tell whether the
specified programme helps them do it.

Every question below is framed around **outcomes** — what result the participant is trying to
achieve — not solutions. If a participant answers with a feature request ("you should have an
app that…"), that is a signal, not an answer: ask what they would be able to do then that they
cannot do now, and follow the answer.

**Do not describe the NTUC Club programme until Section 5.** Everything before that must be
about their life, not our product. If you lead with the programme you will collect polite
approval of it.

**Capture outcome statements in this form:** a direction of improvement plus a unit of
measure — *"minimise the time spent working out whether a reward is worth using"*, *"increase
the number of visits where I feel the place knew me"*. Write them down in the participant's
own words first, then reformat.

---

## Section 1 — Their relationship with the place (10 min)

Warm-up, but load-bearing: this is where the "third place" premise either holds or does not.

1. Tell me about the last time you went to [Downtown East / their clubhouse / the property
   from their screener]. Walk me through the day from arriving to leaving.
2. Who were you with? How typical was that?
3. What made you choose there rather than somewhere else that day?
4. How often does that happen in a month? Is that more or less than a year ago?
5. What would have to be true for you to go more often?
6. When you're there, how much of what you spend is planned before you arrive, and how much
   is decided once you're there?

*Listen for:* whether visits are destination-driven or habitual; who in the group decides and
who pays; whether "the club" is one place or several unconnected ones in their head.

---

## Section 2 — Loyalty programmes they already live with (10 min)

7. Which loyalty or rewards programmes do you actually use — not just belong to?
8. Take the one you use most. What made it stick?
9. Think of one you signed up for and stopped using. What happened?
10. When you're at a till and you're a member of that programme, what do you do? Walk me
    through it physically — where's your phone, what do you say, who does what.
11. Has a programme ever taken away points or expired something on you? What happened, and
    what did you do about it?
12. How do you decide whether points are worth collecting at all?

*Listen for:* the actual mechanics of presenting at a counter (this evidences A004 and H001);
prior experience of expiry (feeds H004); the threshold at which rewards stop feeling worth
the effort (feeds H008). Question 11 is the most valuable in this section — do not rush it.

---

## Section 3 — Value, effort and worth (10 min)

Still no mention of NTUC Club's specific numbers.

13. When you get something back from a place you spend money at, what makes it feel like it
    was worth it versus feels like a token gesture?
14. Think about the last reward or voucher you actually redeemed. What did you have to do to
    get it, and was that effort proportionate?
15. And one you never got round to redeeming — what stopped you?
16. If a place told you you'd earned something, where would you expect to find it? What would
    you do if you couldn't?
17. How do you feel about programmes where the more you spend the more status you get? Does
    that change how you behave?

*Listen for:* the effort/value ratio in their own units; whether status motivates or irritates
(directly feeds G03/H002); where they expect entitlements to live (feeds f6, f8).

---

## Section 4 — Recognition, membership and belonging (10 min)

**For union members, this section is the priority — extend it if time is short elsewhere.**

18. Are you an NTUC union member? *(if yes)* What does that membership get you, in practice?
19. When you think about the benefits you already get from being a union member, which ones do
    you actually use?
20. How did you find out about them?
21. *(all participants)* Has a place you go to regularly ever made it obvious that they know
    you're a regular? What did that look like? How did it land?
22. Is there anywhere you'd say you're treated differently because of who you are or how often
    you go? Is that a good thing?

*Listen for:* whether union benefits are experienced as earned or as background entitlement —
this is the direct test of H002 and A006. If union members describe existing benefits as
"things I've never used", automatic Tier 2 placement will land the same way, and the tier
proposition needs rethinking rather than better communication.

---

## Section 5 — Reacting to the specifics (15 min)

Only now introduce the programme. Present each element plainly, without selling it, and ask
what they'd do — not whether they like it.

23. *"NTUC Club is starting a rewards programme across its clubhouses and Downtown East.
    It's free to join. You'd earn 1 point for every dollar you spend at participating shops
    and restaurants."* — What's your first reaction? What would you want to know next?

24. **Exchange rate — test both scenarios, alternating which you show first between
    participants.** *"1,000 points gets you a $5 voucher."* … *"Another option being
    considered is 1,000 points for a $10 voucher."*
    - For each: what would you have to spend to get there? Is that worth paying attention to?
    - At which of these would you start deliberately choosing NTUC Club places over others?
    - *(This is the evidence base for the client's open rebate-yield decision — RAID I004.
      Record the reaction to each figure separately, not as a preference between them.)*

25. **Expiry.** *"Points would last 12 months from when you earn them, then expire."*
    - What do you make of that?
    - What would you need to know, and when, for that to feel fair rather than a trick?
    - *(Then:)* If you'd been told a month in advance which points were about to go, would
      that change anything? — *tests H004*

26. **Reversal.** *"If you returned something you'd bought, the points for it would come back
    off your balance."*
    - Reasonable or not? What if you'd already spent those points on a voucher?

27. **Tiers.** *"There are two tiers. Everyone starts as a Member. Union members are placed
    into Premium automatically, and others qualify by spending. Both tiers earn points at
    exactly the same rate — Premium gets you extra things like more free parking, day passes
    to the water park and a discount on resort rooms."*
    - What do you make of both tiers earning the same?
    - *(union members)* How does being put into Premium automatically feel?
    - What would make Premium feel like something rather than a label? — *core of G03*

28. **Parking.** *"Members get free parking each month — an hour for Members, two for Premium.
    At launch you'd get a printed coupon rather than it happening automatically at the barrier."*
    - Walk me through how you'd expect to actually use that. — *tests H006*

29. **The card.** *"You'd show a code on your phone at the till, and the shop scans it."*
    - Anything about that you can see going wrong?
    - What would you do if it didn't work — bad signal, flat battery, staff unsure?

---

## Section 6 — Close (5 min)

30. If this programme existed and worked exactly as described, what would be different about
    how you use NTUC Club places? Be honest if the answer is "nothing".
31. Who else in your household would use it? Would you share one membership or have your own?
    — *tests H007*
32. Anything I should have asked you about and didn't?

## Question-framing check

Applied per the outcome-based guidance in `gen-e2-gap-analysis` before finalising. Reframings
already made in this draft:

| Rejected (solution-framed) | Used instead (outcome-framed) |
|---|---|
| "What features would you want in a loyalty app?" | Q5 "What would have to be true for you to go more often?" |
| "Would you use a digital membership card?" | Q10 "When you're at a till… walk me through it physically" |
| "Should we notify you before points expire?" | Q25 "What would you need to know, and when, for that to feel fair?" |
| "Do you like the two-tier structure?" | Q27 "What would make Premium feel like something rather than a label?" |
| "How much should 1,000 points be worth?" | Q24 "What would you have to spend to get there? Is that worth paying attention to?" |

Section 5 necessarily shows concrete stimulus, which is closer to concept testing than pure
JTBD. That is deliberate — the programme's parameters are already fixed and several client
decisions are open, so reactions to specific figures have direct decision value. Keep
Sections 1–4 clean of it, and record Section 5 responses as **reactions to stimulus**, not as
discovered needs, when tagging findings by confidence.

## Consent and handling

Standard research consent; recordings held per PDPA and NTUC Group data governance. Do not
collect NRIC or union membership numbers — union membership is captured as a yes/no
screening attribute only.
